# How it plays out

These are not dramatic breaches. They are the ordinary ways a child's data accumulates and
is acted on, each unremarkable in the moment and consequential in aggregate.

## The profile that starts before memory

It often begins with love. A birth announcement, a first-day-of-school photo, a tagged holiday album,
each posted by an adult, each carrying a name, a date, a place, a face. Over years these settle into a
public record the child never authored. Advertising and analytics code inside the apps of early
childhood adds the behavioural layer. By the time the child is old enough to have an opinion, a profile
already exists, assembled largely by people acting in good faith.

## The engagement loop

A child opens an app built to keep them. Autoplay serves the next thing; a streak punishes a day away;
a notification pulls them back. Each interaction is logged and feeds the tuning of the next. The pattern
is not a single harm but a shaping: a bond with the feed built to keep the child returning, formed at
the age when such patterns set hardest.

## Aggregation into a lasting record

The scattered exposures converge. Data from games, apps, a loyalty scheme, a school platform, and
public posts is combined, by brokers and by the large platforms, into a fuller picture than any single
source holds. Because it is keyed to durable identifiers, a name, a date of birth, a face, a household,
it follows the child forward and compounds rather than expiring.

## From public detail to personal approach

For someone seeking contact, the pattern is patient assembly. A school from a uniform in a photo. A
routine from location tags. A first name and an interest from a public profile. A way in through a
game's chat. Individually harmless, together they let a stranger approach a child in a way that feels
personal and informed. Generative tools make the approach cheaper and more convincing.

## Monitoring that becomes control

Inside the home, a protective tool can slide. A location-sharing app installed for safety becomes a way
to check a teenager is where they said. A parental-control app installed for a young child stays on
into adolescence, now reading messages. The same product, unchanged, has moved from protection to
control, and the child has learned across those years that their screen is never wholly their own. This
is the pattern the [stalkerware check playbook](../../playbooks/stalkerware-check.md) and the
[audit shared accounts playbook](../../playbooks/audit-shared-accounts.md) address from the far side,
once the watching is no longer wanted.
