# Attacks

Extraction reuses a small set of moves. None is exotic, and the interest is in how ordinary
each looks while it runs, and how often one has already been caught in the open with a name
and a number attached, gathered in the [cases](cases/index.rst).

## Collection dressed as a feature

A product whose stated job is one thing gathers and sells on the side: a VPN that logs the
traffic it promised to hide, a torch that asks for location, an antivirus that resells what
it sees. The [Avast case](cases/avast.md) is the clean example, a tracker-blocker whose maker
sold the tracking. The feature is the cover; the data is the business.

## The auction as a data tap

Real-time bidding hands every firm in the auction a description of the reader. A company can
join not to win the advert but to harvest those descriptions, and there is nothing to win or
lose, since the request arrives whether or not a bid follows. The mechanism sold as efficient
ad-buying doubles as a standing feed of who is reading what, where.

## Match, append, enrich

A business hands a broker its own customer list, keyed on the hashed email, and gets it back
thickened with attributes it never gathered: income band, life stage, health interests,
inferred politics. The join key does the joining; the enrichment is simply bought. A thin
first-party record becomes a full profile without the person touching either firm.

## Inferring the protected trait

A special category, health, sexuality, a pregnancy, derived from proxies and sold as a
ready-made audience, with nothing ever ticked as sensitive. [Flo](cases/flo.md) passed the
fact of a logged period; [Grindr](cases/grindr.md) passed the fact of being on a gay dating
app. In each, the sensitive conclusion travelled without a sensitive field.

## The invisible third party

Collection under a first party's brand by a party the person never sees: the embedded SDK,
the tracking pixel. The [Meta Pixel on hospital pages](cases/meta-pixel.md) reported the
booking of a medical appointment to a company the patient had no dealing with, from a website
they had every reason to trust.

## Consent manufactured to order

The banner and the shared consent string produce a record that collection was agreed to,
which is a different thing from agreement. The record is the object of the exercise: it
converts extraction into compliance on paper, and a signature no one meant to give is still a
signature on the file.

## Buying past the warrant

The state joins the market as an ordinary customer and obtains what a court order would
otherwise gate. The [Databroker Files](../surveillance/cases/databroker-files.md) investigation
traced commercially traded location data into the movements of EU and NATO officials. No
interception was needed, only a purchase order. By 2026 the buyers were on the record:
German state police forces, an Austrian ministry and Hungarian services, set out in the
[police in the market](../surveillance/cases/police-in-the-market.md) case, which a state
data protection commissioner reads as circumventing a judicial safeguard rather than meeting
it.

## Resold into the open

A profile does not stay with its first buyer. It is licensed onward, matched into
cooperatives, and, when a broker is breached, spilled into criminal markets where it is
recombined with stolen credentials. An opt-out that might have stopped the first sale seldom
reaches the tenth, by which point no one can say who holds a copy.

## The moves do not change, the reach does

The patterns are old and unglamorous. What moves is the cost of running them: cheaper
matching, sharper inference, wider distribution each year mean the same handful of moves
covers more people, more precisely, than it did the year before. A decade-old technique keeps
producing fresh cases, not because anyone invented a new one, but because the old ones now
reach further for less.
